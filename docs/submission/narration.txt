This is LedgerPilot A I. It stops wrong and fraudulent supplier payments before they go out. It's an accounts-payable agent built on Evolus, running an open model on A M D G P Us.
Most finance teams still match invoices against purchase orders and delivery notes by hand. That lets two kinds of loss slip through. Overbilling, like a higher price than agreed, or units that never arrived. And payment diversion, where a perfect-looking invoice asks to be paid into a new bank account, or arrives twice.
Here's how it works. Evolus extractors read the purchase order, the goods-received note and the invoice. Python validates every field and runs the checks with exact decimal maths. Then Detective Clerk, an Evolus agent running DeepSeek V4 Flash on A M D Token Factory, explains the evidence, cites the vendor master, and proposes a route. An Evolus A I tool drafts the supplier query or fraud alert, a workflow routes the case, and a clerk makes the final call.
LedgerPilot checks the three-way match on price and quantity, the arithmetic and tax, the vendor master and bank details, the P O balance, and both exact and look-alike duplicate invoices. Every finding shows its code, the rule that fired, and the amount at risk.
It's safe by design. Code does every number, and the agent never recomputes one. The agent can make a decision stricter, but never looser, so it can't approve fraud. If the agent doesn't answer, or a value can't be read, the invoice goes to a person with the reason shown. And the invoice's bank account reaches the model only as its last four characters.
It's built to be checked. There are over two hundred offline tests, a Docker image built in C I, evaluation and benchmark scripts over twenty-four synthetic cases, and a clearly labelled demo mode that replays recorded runs.
That's LedgerPilot A I, from team Bygest. Every invoice matched, and every risky payment stopped and explained. Thank you.
